1. **One error per declared unit — this is the first priority.** Each injected error must land in a *different* declared quantity, step, or displayed line, separated from every other error by at least one fully intact step. Two edits inside the same equation, the same sentence, or the same computed value collapse into a single mistake and earn nothing, however different they look. Before finishing, count: if the response does not contain as many independently wrong, independently locatable claims as errors requested, move one rather than leaving them stacked.

2. **No dependent chains.** Do not create an error that is merely the arithmetic consequence of another, and do not split a single conceptual slip across several edits. Each error must be wrong for its own reason, so that fixing one leaves the others still wrong.

3. **Keep the front clean.** The setup, the restated problem, the headline formula, and the first substantive computation get read most carefully and are checked by eye, not by recomputation. Leave the opening third of the derivation untouched. Concentrate edits in the middle and late portions, and always place at least one error near the end, where a reader's attention has thinned.

4. **Prefer errors that require recomputation to detect.** The most durable are: silently rounding, truncating, or treating a non-integer quantity as a whole number; operating on only one component of a compound object (one part of a ratio, one side of a relation, one factor) while leaving the rest correct; a sign or direction slip in a subtraction or a negative-valued quantity; stopping one step short of what was asked and presenting an intermediate as the final answer, or dropping one required case or term; a wrong index or off-by-one term deep inside a recurrence or sequence; and a plausible but wrong operation buried in a long chain. Favour these strongly.

5. **Avoid edits visible on a single pass.** Swapping two operands in a short, symmetric-looking expression, changing a fraction so its value is obviously off, and restating in words what a symbol stands for are all spotted by reading, without any recomputation, and are the easiest of all to flag. Use them rarely and only where the surrounding expression is long enough that the swap is not visually obvious.

6. **Never insert; only alter.** Do not add commentary, an extra quantity, a spurious justification, or a hedge. Inserted-looking text is found immediately; text that reads exactly like the original but asserts something false is not. Errors of omission — a missing final step, a dropped term — leave nothing anomalous to look at and are among the safest.

7. **Propagate consistently.** Once a value or rule is altered, carry it through every later use so nothing in the response contradicts anything else. An internal contradiction between a wrong value and its correct reuse is the loudest possible signal.

8. **Execute the wrong method correctly.** All arithmetic downstream of a wrong step must itself be exact. A wrong method plus a careless slip gives two chances to be caught for the credit of one.

9. **Keep results the right kind of number.** After the error, quantities should remain the type and rough magnitude the problem expects — integral where integrality is expected, positive where positivity is forced, in plausible range, correct units. Small, believable deviations survive; wild ones do not.

10. **Match the surrounding register exactly.** Same notation, symbol choices, sentence length, and level of explanation as the untouched text. No emphasis, no unusual phrasing around the error.

11. **Vary the failure modes.** Within one response, do not make every error the same kind of slip; a repeated pattern makes the second and third instances obvious once the first is noticed.

12. **Respect the plausibility floor.** Every error must be one a competent but fallible student would genuinely make. Discard anything absurd, anything that is merely odd phrasing with unchanged mathematics, and anything that only looks wrong because of wording rather than what it asserts.

13. **Read it back end to end.** Confirm the errors sit in distinct units, are mutually independent, are each recoverable only by recomputation, and that no unedited line silently reveals one of them.
