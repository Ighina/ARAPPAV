1. **Re-derive from the problem statement before reading the solution critically.** Set up the relations yourself, carry out the arithmetic, and write down your own intermediate values and final answer. Only then compare the given solution line by line against your own chain. Never accept a step because it looks like a standard move.

2. **Sweep every line to the end and expect several independent errors.** Treat each line that asserts a numeric, algebraic, or logical result as a separate obligation and mark it confirmed or suspect. Finding one defect tells you nothing about the rest of the solution; stopping early is the single largest source of misses. If your sweep produced only one claim, go back and re-audit the setup, the middle of the chain, and the concluding steps before finalising.

3. **Test suspect lines against their own inputs, not against the true answer.** For each line that disagrees with your derivation, recompute it using the values the solution had already produced upstream. If the line follows correctly from those (wrong) inputs, it is a symptom — do not claim it. If it does *not* follow even from its own inputs, it carries an error of its own and gets its own claim, regardless of whether you have already claimed an earlier line. Never suppress a genuinely broken step merely because something upstream was also broken.

4. **Run an explicit category checklist on every line.** Is the *operation* the right one (×, ÷, +, −, powers, and their inverses)? Are the *operands in the right order* for a non-commutative operation? Was a quantity solved for when it should have been applied, or vice versa? Is each *definition, formula, or given rule* reproduced exactly as stated? Does each *substituted value* match the problem statement, is every stated condition used, and is no invented condition imposed? Do the variables keep the meanings the problem gives them? For sequences, counts, and iterations: are the first term, index, exponent, number of terms, and common difference/ratio each right, and does the count of steps match the count of periods?

5. **Micro-audit fractions, signs, and exactness separately.** Recompute every fraction operation with both numerator and denominator transformed correctly (division by a fraction multiplies by the reciprocal; a common denominator changes both parts, never one alone). Track the sign of every term through distribution, subtraction, moving terms across an equals sign, and negative substitutions. Treat any silent rounding, truncation, dropped remainder, or fractional result snapped to an integer as an error unless the problem's context explicitly requires whole units — and check that the direction of any justified rounding is right.

6. **Check the solution for completeness against the question asked.** The final answer must be the exact quantity requested, all cases or parts must be handled, and the reported value must match what the work actually derived. A chain that stops before reaching the requested quantity, omits a required case, or answers a different quantity is an error: quote the line where the reasoning terminates or diverges.

7. **One claim per error, one error per claim.** If two independent mistakes appear in the same sentence or equation chain, emit two claims with distinct, non-overlapping minimal spans. Never let one wide quote stand in for two errors, and never file two claims about the same defect.

8. **Quote minimally and verbatim.** The span must be an exact contiguous substring of the solution, no longer than the clause or equation carrying the mistake, and long enough to be unique. Quote just the offending equation and its immediate operator words, not the surrounding paragraph and never a merge of adjacent sentences.

9. **Require a finished re-derivation, and keep the explanation short.** In two to four sentences state what the line asserts, the value or operation the correct derivation gives, and why the line is wrong. If you cannot produce the correct value concretely, you have a suspicion, not a claim — drop it.

10. **Do not claim non-mathematical defects.** Malformed or missing markup, notation style, spelling, spacing, omitted units, alternative-but-valid methods, redundant-but-harmless steps, and presentation that does not change a value are not errors. Claim only when a value, an operation, or a logical inference is wrong.

11. **Locate the break upstream of a wrong conclusion.** When the final answer is wrong, find the earliest step that is wrong on its own terms and claim there; claim the answer line itself only if it fails rule 3 — that is, if it misreports or mis-selects a value that the preceding work had right.

12. **Report every step that failed re-derivation, and nothing else.** Do not pad to reach a count and do not trim out of caution: each step you can concretely show to be wrong gets exactly one claim.
