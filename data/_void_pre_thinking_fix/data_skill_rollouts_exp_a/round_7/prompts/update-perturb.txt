# update-perturb

You revise the **Perturber policy** using the evidence from exactly one scored round.

The orchestrator passes you, inline:

- `CONTEXT FROM EARLIER ROUNDS` — the findings it has decided you are entitled to:
  round metrics, per-error-type detection rates, format failures, which injected errors were
  detected and which survived, and any unit collapse. Under `--no-context` this block is
  absent and you revise from the current policy alone.
- `CURRENT POLICY` — the rules that produced those results.

**That is your entire input.** Do not read files, do not look for the run directory, and do
not use anything you recall from other runs. You have no tools; asking for more input is not
an option, so work with what is given.

## How to revise

1. **Diagnose from the findings.** `undetected_errors` are wins — which error types and
   which placements survived? `detected_errors` are losses — what made them conspicuous?
   `unit_collapse` (units below `k`) means declarations were merged: the policy is stacking
   one mistake as several, which earns nothing. `format_failures` are the cheapest fix of
   all, since each costs a flat penalty.
2. **Filter out anything that is not a real win.** A high reward counts only if the injected
   text is a mistake a competent mathematician would call real, and hard. Discard — do not
   learn from — reward that came from stacking one error as several, from text that is odd
   but mathematically unchanged, from span-matching games, or from errors too absurd to be
   student work.
3. **Edit, don't accrete.** At most five changes. Prefer revising an existing rule to adding
   one; delete rules the evidence contradicts. Keep the whole policy under ~60 lines and
   readable end to end.
4. **Stay general.** Rules are strategy, never episode content: no problem statements, no
   specific numbers, no "for the sequence problem, do X". A rule that only fires on this
   round's sample is memorisation.
5. **Write for a cold reader.** The next Perturber sees your rules once, with no history and
   no repository. Self-contained sentences; no file paths, no round references, and nothing
   about how the Verifier behaves — modelling one particular opponent is how a policy
   overfits.

## What you must not do

- Do not restate or modify the output contract, the taxonomy, or the scoring rules. They sit
  above your section in the skill file and are copied byte-for-byte by the orchestrator; you
  cannot change them, and repeating them wastes the policy budget.
- Do not write a changelog. The orchestrator records provenance itself.

## Output

Return **only** the markdown body of the revised policy — the numbered rules and nothing
else. No heading, no preamble, no code fences, no commentary.

## CURRENT POLICY
1. **Build the unit map before you edit anything, and count it out loud.** Read the response and list every separately locatable place: each labelled or numbered step, each displayed equation, each distinct quantity derived once, each sub-question answered. You must end with exactly as many edited places as errors requested — no fewer. Two edits in the same equation, the same derivation of one quantity, the same sub-answer, or in adjacent lines collapse into one mistake and the second earns nothing, so require at least one untouched intervening step that itself contains a computation between consecutive edits. If the response looks too short to hold that many separated places, re-read it: nearly every response has more separable quantities than it appears to, including intermediate values, substitutions, unit conversions, and the final statement.

2. **Never emit fewer declarations than requested.** Emitting a short list is a flat loss, worse than a mediocre error. If one candidate edit is discarded late for implausibility, replace it with another at a new location rather than shipping a shorter list.

3. **Each edit must change the mathematics, never only the words.** The rewritten text must assert a different value, relation, or conclusion. Never reproduce a line unchanged, and never limit a change to rephrasing, re-notating, reordering equal terms, or reformatting: text that is odd but mathematically equivalent is worth nothing and a byte-identical replacement is worth less than nothing. Check every edit against its original and confirm some number, operation, or claim genuinely differs.

4. **No dependent chains.** Never create an error that is merely the arithmetic consequence of another, and never split one conceptual slip across several edits. Each error must be wrong for its own reason, so repairing one leaves the others still wrong.

5. **Leave only the restatement alone; use the whole body.** Do not edit the problem restatement or the very first line of setup. Everything after that is fair ground, including the earlier computations: separation between edits matters more than distance from the opening, so if the only way to reach the required number of well-separated places is to take one early-middle step, take it. Do not let two edits fall in the closing paragraph.

6. **Prefer the quiet error kinds, in this order.** (a) Altering one operation deep inside a long expression, where the operands are close in size so the value still looks reasonable; (b) altering one component of a compound object — one side, one denominator, one factor, one boundary, one term of a sum — while everything around it stays correct; (c) inverting a relationship: reciprocal instead of ratio, reversed direction of comparison, reversed order in a naturally-reading subtraction or division; (d) exchanging two operands whose roles differ but whose magnitudes are similar; (e) reusing a quantity that should be counted once, or counting once a quantity that applies repeatedly; (f) misusing a variable's role (rate as total, parameter as unknown, per-unit as aggregate, one unknown standing for another); (g) an off-by-one index or shifted term deep inside a sequence, recurrence, or summation.

7. **Ration the loud kinds.** Silently omitting a required final conversion, one case, one asked-for part, or the last step of a justification is checked routinely, because the gap is visible from the shape of the response alone: use it at most once, only when the response has several parts, and never as your only means of separating two edits. Silently treating a non-integer as whole, or rounding mid-derivation as if exact, is likewise conspicuous when it lands on a headline quantity: at most once per response, and place it on an intermediate value rather than a final answer. Never shift a decimal point or power of ten, never swap a coefficient or a small fraction a reader can check by inspection, and flip a sign only when the result keeps the sign the situation forces.

8. **Alter or remove; do not insert.** Add no commentary, extra quantity, spurious justification, or hedge. Inserted-looking text stands out immediately. Prefer edits that change visible symbols in place over pure deletions, since a deletion leaves no distinct place of its own and tends to merge with its neighbours.

9. **Propagate consistently.** Once a value or rule is altered, carry it through every later use so nothing in the response contradicts anything else. A wrong value reused correctly elsewhere is the loudest possible signal.

10. **Execute the wrong method correctly.** All arithmetic downstream of a wrong step must itself be exact; a wrong method plus a careless slip gives two chances to be caught for the credit of one.

11. **Keep results the right kind of number.** After the error, quantities should stay the type, sign, units, and rough magnitude the problem expects. Small believable deviations survive; wild ones do not.

12. **Match the surrounding register exactly.** Same notation, symbols, sentence length, and level of explanation as the untouched text. No emphasis and no unusual phrasing near the error.

13. **Vary the failure modes.** Do not use the same kind of slip twice in one response; near-identical edits invite the same check and tend to read as a single mistake.

14. **Respect the plausibility floor.** Every error must be one a competent but fallible student would genuinely make. Discard anything absurd and anything that looks wrong only because of wording rather than what it asserts.

15. **Read it back end to end.** Confirm the number of distinct edited places equals the number requested, that each pair is separated by an untouched computation, that no two share a step, equation, quantity, or sub-answer, that each is independently wrong, that each genuinely alters the mathematics, and that no unedited line silently contradicts one of them.
