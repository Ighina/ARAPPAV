## My own derivation

The inequality is $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} + |z| \leq 1$.

Let $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ (the cylindrical radius). Then the inequality becomes:
$$r + |z| \leq 1$$

This means $|z| \leq 1 - r$.

For this to be valid, we need $1 - r \geq 0$, so $r \leq 1$.

For a fixed value of $r$ (where $0 \leq r \leq 1$):
- $|z| \leq 1 - r$ means $-(1-r) \leq z \leq 1-r$
- The height in the $z$-direction is $2(1-r)$

The volume can be computed using cylindrical coordinates:
$$V = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 \int_{-(1-r)}^{1-r} r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$$

First integrate with respect to $z$:
$$\int_{-(1-r)}^{1-r} dz = 2(1-r)$$

So:
$$V = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 r \cdot 2(1-r) \, dr \, d\theta$$

$$V = 2\pi \int_0^1 2r(1-r) \, dr = 2\pi \int_0^1 (2r - 2r^2) \, dr$$

$$V = 2\pi \left[ r^2 - \frac{2r^3}{3} \right]_0^1 = 2\pi \left(1 - \frac{2}{3}\right) = 2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2\pi}{3}$$
